Broken storefront glass in Washington, DC: what to do first

A shattered display window needs a clear plan: keep customers away, identify whether the opening is exposed and call about securing it.

Keep people away from the damaged opening

Move customers and staff away from loose glass. Avoid using a damaged glass door or walking beneath a cracked transom. Use another safe entrance when available and tell your site manager which area is affected.

Describe the opening, not just the crack

From a safe position, note whether the damage affects a display pane, door lite, sidelite or overhead panel. Tell us whether glass is still in the frame and whether the business can be secured. Do not touch the pane to check how firmly it is held.

Decide whether temporary protection comes first

An exposed opening may need emergency board-up before a replacement pane is ready. Board-up and permanent glass replacement solve different problems. Ask what the immediate secure-up covers and how the next visit will be planned.

Prepare the next conversation

Send a safe-distance photo, address or ZIP, business or home, urgency and on-site contact. Note access or landlord approval requirements. Ask for an available attendance window and a separate replacement estimate once glass and measurements are confirmed.

Questions before you call

Do I need to know which glass broke?

No. Describe the location of the panel and send a safe photo. The replacement specification must be confirmed before ordering.

Does a board-up include permanent replacement?

Ask for the scope of each stage. Temporary protection and glass replacement may involve separate materials, work and scheduling.
