Board-up after a storefront break-in in Washington, DC

Once it is safe to return, an exposed storefront needs a plan for temporary protection and permanent glass replacement.

Glass. Board-up. Door hardware.

One number to discuss your next step.

Wait until the site is safe to enter

Follow the directions of emergency responders where involved. Keep staff and customers away from damaged glazing and disturbed entrances. Avoid handling glass, touching damaged hardware or disturbing an area that has not been cleared.

Describe every affected part of the entrance

A broken pane may come with a bent frame, damaged lock cylinder or door that will no longer latch. Share those observations from a safe position so glass, door and locksmith needs can be discussed together.

Treat board-up as a temporary stage

Ask how the opening can be covered while the replacement specification is checked. Temporary panels do not establish that every entrance, lock or security system is functioning. Discuss the remaining vulnerabilities with your property manager.

Keep a record and confirm approval

Take photos only when safe, preserve invoices and contact your insurer about its requirements. Coverage is policy-specific. Identify the owner, tenant or manager who can approve the glass and hardware scope before work is arranged.

Questions before you call

Can locks be checked along with the glass?

Describe lock, latch and frame damage when you call. Glass repair, commercial door hardware and locksmith services can be discussed together.

Will insurance cover the work?

Only your insurer can confirm coverage, exclusions, deductibles and claim requirements. Keep safe photos and invoices and ask the insurer what documentation is needed.
